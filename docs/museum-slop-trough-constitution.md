# Museum — Slop Trough Constitution

## Status

Permanent ITEM product invariant.

This document defines the irreducible duality of the entire public experience:

```txt
MUSEUM — SLOP TROUGH
```

Everything else may evolve. This polarity must remain visible, legible, and structurally true.

## Constitutional Principle

ITEM is not primarily a social network, marketplace, image generator, or content feed.

It is a two-part cultural instrument:

```txt
The Museum preserves what deserves time.
The Slop Trough exposes what appetite produces without judgment.
```

The two sides are not separate brands accidentally sharing infrastructure. They are reciprocal chambers of one system.

The Museum has meaning because the Trough exists.

The Trough has diagnostic value because the Museum proves that selection, care, and permanence are still possible.

## What the Museum Means

The Museum is not merely a clean visual style.

It is an institutional philosophy built from:

- selection
- custody
- scholarship
- preservation
- provenance
- interpretation
- public trust
- ritualized attention
- resistance to time

A museum does not say that everything is equally worthy.

It says:

```txt
This was examined.
This was situated.
This was preserved for a reason.
```

## What the Slop Trough Means

The Slop Trough is not merely an ugly page.

It is the visible condition of synthetic abundance without sufficient judgment:

- endless generation
- imitation without lineage
- appetite without memory
- speed without reflection
- engagement without consequence
- images detached from process
- repetition mistaken for culture

It must remain seductive enough to be recognizable, but unmistakably consumptive.

The Trough is diagnosis, satire, containment, contrast, and evidence.

It must never become the application's primary addiction engine.

## Museum Lineage

The ITEM Museum should inherit the strongest philosophical and aesthetic lessons from the history of museums without copying any single institution.

### 1. Temple and Treasury

Inherit:

- threshold
- procession
- reverence
- controlled revelation
- material permanence
- the feeling that entry changes behavior

Product translation:

- a deliberate entrance
- restrained motion
- no immediate infinite feed
- works revealed as encounters, not inventory tiles

### 2. Studiolo and Cabinet of Curiosities

Inherit:

- intimate discovery
- unusual classification
- drawers, indexes, and hidden relationships
- wonder joined to scholarship

Product translation:

- lineage maps
- artifact families
- symbolic taxonomies
- curator drawers
- cross-links between strange objects and ideas

### 3. Enlightenment and Public Museum

Inherit:

- public access
- cataloging
- comparative study
- ambition to organize knowledge
- legible educational interpretation

Product translation:

- accession records
- searchable canon
- clear labels
- public research routes
- open explanations of why an artifact matters

### 4. Salon and Comparative Exhibition

Inherit:

- argument through juxtaposition
- public judgment
- competing schools and styles
- curation as an active intellectual act

Reject:

- indiscriminate visual crowding
- popularity as the only measure

Product translation:

- paired comparisons
- canon trials
- curator arguments
- visible reasons for acceptance or rejection

### 5. Modern Gallery and the White Cube

Inherit:

- silence
- negative space
- controlled light
- object primacy
- visual restraint

Reject:

- sterile emptiness without interpretation

Product translation:

- one primary object at a time
- generous margins
- minimal interface chrome
- no competing notification clutter

### 6. Archive and Memorial Museum

Inherit:

- witness
- ethical gravity
- documentary evidence
- refusal to detach objects from consequence
- preservation of difficult context

Product translation:

- complete provenance
- process history
- revision records
- disclosure of human and machine roles
- durable audit trails

### 7. Natural-History and Science Museum

Inherit:

- specimen logic
- systems thinking
- diagrams
- classification
- scale comparison
- visible methods of inquiry

Product translation:

- modal DNA
- artifact anatomy
- technical notes
- lineage graphs
- comparable artifact classes

### 8. Contemporary and Media Museum

Inherit:

- living interpretation
- multimodal exhibition
- participation
- conservation of software, sound, film, and interactive systems

Product translation:

- Full-Mode Artifacts
- time-based media
- interactive exhibits
- version preservation
- executable and experiential artifacts

## Historical Correction

ITEM must inherit museum rigor without inheriting museum denial.

Great museums also carry histories of extraction, conquest, false attribution, exclusion, and decontextualization.

Therefore the ITEM Museum must require:

- transparent provenance
- source disclosure
- attribution
- visible uncertainty
- correction history
- contestable interpretation
- no silent appropriation of a creator's process

The Museum must be authoritative without pretending to be infallible.

## Spatial Model

The website should behave like architecture, not like a conventional feed.

```txt
Threshold
→ Vestibule
→ Gallery
→ Object encounter
→ Wall label
→ Dossier
→ Lineage room
→ Study room
→ Archive
→ Conservation / refinement chamber
```

The Slop Trough is the counter-architecture:

```txt
Opening
→ stream
→ saturation
→ repetition
→ appetite
→ exhaustion
→ possible extraction into critique or refinement
```

## Persistent Duality

The user should always know which chamber they occupy.

### Museum indicators

- accession number
- room or collection name
- curator context
- provenance status
- canon status
- restrained navigation
- finite exhibition sequence

### Slop Trough indicators

- trough status
- source ambiguity warnings
- repetition / derivative signals
- low-provenance markers
- visible consumption metrics used diagnostically, not aspirationally
- exit routes into critique, refinement, or quarantine

## Entry Gesture

The opening choice remains foundational:

```txt
MUSEUM
Enter what has survived judgment.

SLOP TROUGH
Enter what generation produced without enough judgment.
```

This is not a theme selector.

It is the governing philosophical choice of the experience.

## Museum Aesthetic Constitution

### Material language

- stone
- paper
- glass
- oxidized metal
- blackened steel
- bronze
- restrained gold
- archival cloth
- dark wood used sparingly

### Color behavior

Museum:

- black
- bone
- limestone
- parchment
- charcoal
- muted metal
- selective gold as a mark of canon, never decoration everywhere

Slop Trough:

- synthetic saturation
- wet gradients
- cheap fluorescence
- compressed contrast
- unstable color
- seductive visual excess

### Typography

Museum:

- an authoritative serif for titles and institutional language
- a neutral sans serif for labels, metadata, and controls
- accession-number typography
- generous leading
- no novelty type as the default

Slop Trough:

- compressed display type
- degraded interface fragments
- excessive counters and labels used critically
- legibility must remain sufficient for accessibility

### Motion

Museum:

- slow
- purposeful
- reversible
- quiet
- motion used to reveal structure

Slop Trough:

- fast
- looping
- repetitive
- excessive by design

Neither side may violate accessibility settings or trap the user.

### Sound

Museum defaults to silence.

Sound appears only when an artifact requires it.

The Trough may suggest cacophony, but audio must remain opt-in and controllable.

## Object Presentation

An ITEM artifact should not appear as a generic social card.

It should appear as a museum object with:

- accession ID
- title
- date
- creator / contributors
- materials and media
- human inputs
- machine transformations
- dimensions or runtime
- collection
- canon state
- provenance confidence
- curator statement
- criticism / dissent
- lineage
- conservation notes where relevant

## Pacing Law

Museum pacing is finite and intentional.

```txt
No infinite scroll as the primary Museum experience.
```

Permitted:

- rooms
- chapters
- exhibition sequences
- curated shelves
- search results
- finite collection pages

The Museum should create stopping points.

The Trough may represent endlessness, but it must still include deliberate exits and safeguards against exploitative engagement design.

## Curatorial Law

The Museum is not a popularity contest.

Public signals may inform judgment, but canon requires:

- provenance
- examination
- critique
- comparative context
- curator accountability
- reasons recorded in public

A highly liked work can remain outside canon.

An initially unpopular work can enter canon if the argument for preservation is strong.

## Monetization Law

The Museum monetizes:

- preservation
- membership
- patronage
- editions
- dossiers
- research access
- exhibitions
- provenance services
- conservation and refinement tools

The Slop Trough monetizes only legitimate diagnostic value:

- anti-slop analysis
- synthetic-media audits
- creator critique
- dataset-quality tools
- refinement pathways

The Slop Trough must not monetize compulsive consumption as its core business.

## Product Invariants

These rules survive every redesign:

```txt
1. Museum and Slop Trough remain the two primary chambers.
2. The Museum never becomes an infinite synthetic feed.
3. The Trough never masquerades as canon.
4. Every Museum artifact carries provenance and interpretive context.
5. Submission is easy; publication is selective.
6. Canon decisions record reasons.
7. Public popularity is signal, not truth.
8. Human and machine roles are disclosed.
9. The interface creates attention rather than harvesting it.
10. Monetization cannot destroy the distinction it depends upon.
```

## Feature Test

Every proposed feature must answer four questions:

```txt
Which chamber does this belong to?
Does it strengthen the contrast?
Does it increase judgment, provenance, or preservation?
Could it accidentally turn the Museum into the Trough?
```

A feature that cannot answer the first question should not ship.

## Acceptance Criteria for the Site

The duality is successfully implemented when:

- the landing page presents the two chambers clearly
- the Museum feels institutional rather than feed-like
- the Slop Trough feels consumptive without becoming the product's addiction engine
- artifact pages resemble cataloged objects rather than posts
- navigation resembles rooms, collections, dossiers, and archives
- approved works alone enter Museum exhibition routes
- rejected or low-signal works are quarantined, refined, or routed to the Trough with transparent rules
- users can always cross the threshold intentionally rather than drifting invisibly between modes

## Final Compression

```txt
MUSEUM — SLOP TROUGH

Preservation against appetite.
Judgment against generation without end.
Memory against the feed.
```

The site may become richer, stranger, more technical, and more commercial.

It must never stop being this dual structure.
